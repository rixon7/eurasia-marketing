It's a fair question. Digital marketing gets talked about constantly, agencies pop up everywhere, and yet for many business owners, it's still not entirely clear what an agency actually does on a day-to-day basis — or why it might be worth paying for.

Let's clear that up.

## The Full Scope of What a Digital Marketing Agency Does

A full-service digital marketing agency covers a wide range of specialisms, often working across several of them simultaneously for each client.

**Strategy** is where everything begins. Before any campaign is launched or content is written, a good agency takes time to understand your business, your customers, and your goals. The strategy determines which channels to focus on, what the messaging should be, and how success will be measured.

**Search Engine Optimisation (SEO)** is the process of improving your website so it ranks higher in Google search results. This involves technical improvements to your site, creating relevant content, and building the authority of your domain through links and citations. For local businesses, local SEO specifically targets customers searching for services in your area.

**Social media management** involves creating and publishing content on platforms like Instagram, Facebook, LinkedIn, and TikTok. This includes writing captions, designing graphics, responding to comments, and managing your community. Done well, it builds brand awareness and keeps your business front of mind.

**Paid advertising** (also known as PPC — pay per click) covers Google Ads and social media advertising on platforms like Facebook, Instagram, and LinkedIn. Unlike organic marketing, paid ads generate immediate visibility. A skilled agency manages your budget carefully to maximise the return on every pound spent.

**Web design and development** covers everything from building a new website to improving an existing one. Your website is the hub of all your marketing activity, and a slow, outdated, or confusing site will undermine every other effort you make.

**Content marketing** involves creating valuable, relevant content — blog posts, videos, guides, case studies — that attracts your target audience and builds trust over time. It supports both SEO and social media.

**Email marketing** manages your subscriber list, creates campaigns, sets up automations, and tracks performance to keep your audience engaged and drive repeat business.

**Reporting and analytics** ties everything together. A good agency tracks what's working, explains it clearly, and uses the data to continuously improve results.

## In-House vs Agency: What's the Difference?

Hiring a full-time, in-house marketing employee gives you dedicated attention but significant cost — salary, national insurance, pension, holiday cover, and the limitation that one person rarely has deep expertise across all the disciplines listed above.

An agency gives you access to a team of specialists — strategists, SEO experts, designers, copywriters, and ad managers — for a fraction of the cost of hiring each of them individually. You get breadth and depth without the overhead.

For most small and medium-sized businesses, an agency is significantly more cost-effective than building an in-house team, particularly in the early stages of growth.

## What to Expect When Working With an Agency

The working relationship varies by agency, but at a minimum you should expect regular communication, transparent reporting, and a clear explanation of what is being done and why.

A good agency won't blind you with jargon or hide behind complex dashboards. They'll speak to you in plain terms, tell you honestly when something isn't working, and proactively suggest improvements.

You should expect to be involved, particularly at the start. The agency needs to understand your business deeply to represent it well — that requires your input on your customers, your values, your tone of voice, and your goals.
